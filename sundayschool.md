CHARACTER
Memory Verse: Prov 4:23

This lesson is very important to carefully digest because many people who ought to be in heaven are in hell today due to character; many families lack joy, harmony, and unity, while many households have broken apart. The prayers of some are blocked because of character. What is worse, some have lost their daily job, while others turned a benefactor into an enemy, thus driving that benefactor away.

What Character Means: Character is what reveals the kind of person someone truly is. Prov 4:13-27, 6:16-19, 14:1-2, 26:20-28, 27:3-4, 15, 22-23, 22:24-25, 2:19, 20:19-22.

TYPES OF CHARACTER THAT EXIST

1.	BAD CHARACTER

Cruelty, anger, malice, stubbornness, rebellion, ruthlessness, pride, corruption, filthiness, fighting, backbiting, gossip, disrespect, eye-service, partiality, untruthfulness, hypocrisy, repaying evil for good, murder, drunkenness, lack of interest in good works, fornication, adultery, idolatry, worldliness, and double-mindedness. Prov 17:13, Heb 3:2-12.

These behaviors sprout from the human heart Lk 6:45, Matt 15:18-20, Jer 13:23.
It can also be inherited through parents' bloodline Ezek 16:44-45. It can come from chosen friends Prov 22:24, 1 Cor 15:33, 2 Sam 13:1-14, Isa 6:5-6. It can also develop through cohabitants, coworkers, and neighbors, or prevalent cultural customs that can easily creep into a believer's life Ps 120:5-7, Jer 10:2, Isa 33:1, Jer 5:25-26.

DANGERS OF BAD CHARACTER
Prov 17:13, Ps 109:1-21, Heb 3:7-19, 1 Kings 14:7-11, 16:3-20.

Bad character blocks a person's benefactor, promotion, and mercy; it leads to regret, losing one's wife, unexpected lack, poverty, incurable diseases, open doors for spiritual attacks, and premature death. Dishonesty keeps a person trapped in poverty and lack, while preventing them from being fruitful in their labor John 15:1-6, 16. Above all, it causes a person to lose their glorious heavenly home Matt 7:21-23.

THE WAY OUT — Isaiah 55:6–7, Acts 3:19–20

Repentance, prayer, deliverance from the bondage of evil character that dwells in the heart and blood. Romans 7:15–24, Hebrews 12:4,1 Thessalonians 5:22–24, Hebrews 12:1–4, Romans 12:1–2.

A life of self-control — Job 31:1, 1 Thessalonians 4:1–5, 1 John 5:18.

(2) GODLIKE CHARACTER (GOOD CHARACTER) — Ephesians 5:1–7, 1 Timothy 6:3–11, 1 Peter 1:5–9, 2 Peter 1:4

Godlike character means having the heart to behave according to GOD'S CHARACTER. This is impossible without first experiencing a change of heart through repentance turning away from evil character into a Godlike character. Acts 3:19–20, Isaiah 55:6–9, 2 Corinthians 5:17.

GODLIKE CHARACTER — John 3:16, 13:34–35, 15:12–14, Matthew 5:45 (Love for all people: 1 John 4:7–11); Holiness: 1 Peter 1:15–16; Truthfulness/Faithfulness: Numbers 23:19, Joshua 21:45; Merciful: Exodus 34:6–7.

When a believer experiences redemption, a heart of Godlike character will begin to manifest from within, which people will notice. 1 Peter 1:5–9, 1 Corinthians 11:1, John 2:6.

Discussion Time: Let the Pastor give room for people to share briefly about the end of those who have this evil character around them.

BEING A PARTAKER OF GOD'S DIVINE NATURE

Bible Reading: 2 Peter 1:1–10

Memory Verse: 1 Timothy 6:6

The lessons we have been learning clearly reveal that evil character is bad and its outcome carries great danger.

Today’s lesson teaches us that we need to be partakers of God’s divine nature (Verse 4). How is God's character revealed to us? Although we do not see God face-to-face, He sent Jesus His Son to reveal God's character to us through His own life and teachings — Matthew 5:42–48.

This lesson is divided into two parts:

1. GOD'S INCOMMUNICABLE ATTRIBUTES (ATTRIBUTES WE CANNOT SHARE WITH GOD)

Because He is the Creator of all things (a consuming fire, Judge of all men, He can kill, He can make alive, He can exalt, and He can bring down; Immortality, Unchangeable, Incorruptible, Omnipresent, All-Hearing, Omniscient who sees in the dark and knows what happens in the depths of the earth) — Isaiah 40:18, 43:9–11, 25–26, 46:9–10, 40:28–31, Hebrews 12:28–29, Psalm 50:1–14, Romans 14:10–12. God did not create man to share these particular attributes with Him because He is the supreme authority over all creation.

2. GOD'S COMMUNICABLE ATTRIBUTES (ATTRIBUTES WE CAN SHARE WITH HIM)

Love for all people, self-control, endurance, perseverance, being a source of blessing to all people, goodness, mercy, helpfulness, guiding the lost, showing appreciation to people, forgiving those who offend us, compassion, being faithful and holy, unblemished — Philippians 2:15, 1 Timothy 6:6–11, Exodus 34:6–8, John 3:16–18, Romans 5:8–11, 1 John 4:7–21.

PARTAKING OF GOD'S DIVINE NATURE: JOSEPH AS AN EXAMPLE OF GODLIKE CHARACTER

Genesis 45:1–5, 2 Peter 1:4–11, 1 Peter 5:1–7

In the continuation of our lesson on Godlike character today, we want to look at the life of Joseph in alignment with this, so that we can see that it is truly possible for mortal human beings like us to possess genuine Godlike character if we yield our hearts completely to God and discipline ourselves.

Consider the comparison of Godlike character in the life of Joseph — Genesis 45:1–5, Luke 19:10, a vision to save many lives. Just as the Jews hated Jesus and crucified Him without Him committing any sin, Joseph similarly faced persecution from his brothers. his brothers, which ultimately resulted in the salvation of many during the time of famine — Genesis 37:18–20, 50:20–21, Isaiah 53:1–5, Hebrews 5:4–10, 1 John 2:6, John 15:18–25.

Joseph suffered for the sake of righteousness and his future good vision; he forgave those who rose up as his adversaries, just as Jesus the Lord did — Genesis 39:1–2, 45:1–5, Luke 6:21–23, 35–37, 1 Peter 2:11–25, 3:8–17.

Joseph demonstrated love to his enemies, just as Jesus the Lord taught us to have agape love — Matthew 5:43–48.

Joseph did not report the wicked deeds of his brothers to any of the Egyptians, not even up to Pharaoh; he exercised patience, just as Jesus taught us — Romans 12:17–21.

Jesus forgave our sins and cleansed us with His blood so that we could become acceptable before God into eternal life — 2 Corinthians 5:17–21, John 14:1–2, 1 John 3:1–3. What steps are you taking to forgive those who have offended you, or to make peace between you and those you have offended? Make amends today before the day grows too late. Finally, have your relatives heard the gospel of Jesus from your mouth? What steps have you taken so that your father, mother, younger ones, and elder brothers can come into eternal life through your conduct, extending Christ's love to them, calling them to the gathering of the children of God, and praying intercessory prayers for them? — Romans 9:1–4, 10:1–2, 14–17.

The apostles of Jesus laid the foundation of Jesus' love in their time as the Holy Spirit guided them to write in their epistles to the church of God scattered everywhere (Romans 12:9, 1 Thessalonians 3:12, 1 John 4:7).

The Church of God is a place where love should exist according to the setup of God's word. It is a place where those who desire to benefit from love receive satisfaction for their soul's longing (Romans 13:13, John 3:16, Acts 2:41–47).

CHRIST, THE HEAD OF THE CHURCH, AN EXAMPLE OF TRUE LOVE — Romans 5:6–12, Ephesians 5:23–27, John 10:10–11, 17–18, 13:33–35

5.	Sharing in joy or sorrow among brethren — Romans 12:10, 13–16.

6.	Offering intercessory prayer for one another — Acts 12:1–12.

7.	Searching for backsliders in the faith — 1 John 2:1–2.

9.	Making practical use of the opportunity for deep fellowship to hear God's word, receive prayers, and visit those who were not seen in the place of worship — Hebrews 10:24–25.

The word of God says serve one another in love — Philippians 2:25–30, 1 Peter 4:8–10.

Whatever a believer does in ministry that does not spring from a heart of love is not acceptable to God — 1 Samuel 16:7, 1 Timothy 1:5.

THE POSITION OF JESUS IN THE TRINITY

Gen. 1:1, 26, 3:22, 11:6-7, Deut. 6:4-16, Mat. 28:19, 1 Jh. 5:7-11, 1Kor. 8:5, Jh. 10:30-38, 8:28-32, Kol. 1:14-16.

Before birth, Jesus the Lord was opposed by a group called the Sadducees; these people did not believe in doctrines such as the Trinity, the Resurrection of the dead, and Angels Acts 23:8-12, Mat. 22:23-31.

1. MEANING OF GOD IN THREE PERSONS (THE TRINITY)

The doctrine of the Trinity is a mystery beyond human understanding. In the book of Gen. 1:1, the God present at creation is called ELOHIM in the Hebrew language, which means more than one Person. Likewise, these verses show it: Gen. 1:26, 3:22, 11:6-7, 1Jh. 6:7-11, along with Mat. 28:19, which means God the Father, God the Son whom we call Jesus, and God the Holy Spirit. Existing from the beginning, these three are one and the same. (See these verses: Jh. 10:30, 14:8-11, Acts 5:3-4, Eph. 4:30, Heb. 10:29).

Things we can use to illustrate the Trinity are like a radio or a handset, which uses a single battery as its power source and has one general brand name like Nokia, yet its functions are more than one. We can receive calls, send out text messages, receive music, take photos, listen to radio, download, and browse, provided the battery, SIM card, and credit are present. Similarly, our Trinity is in unity with the same power from the time of creation, for salvation, healing, and calling for ministry Exod. 3:7-10, Ezek. 3:17-20, Lev. 18:1-5, 1 Sam. 16:1-3.

Jesus the Son of God became flesh through Mary to save sinners. We saw Him face to face; He became the friend of sinners, came to shed His blood, laid down His life for the sins of the world, and came to seek those who were lost so that He could save them Jh. 10:10-15. This makes us understand that He convicts the heart of a sinner so that the Holy Spirit becomes Jesus' representative to give us understanding. He also bears witness in us to establish salvation up to Calvary Jh. 15:1-8, 16, Lk. 24:46-53, and continually calls the chosen for the advancement of the gospel ministry Rom. 8:15-16, Jh. 16:7-9, Acts 13:1-4.

2. THE POSITION OF JESUS IN THE TRINITY - Jh. 10:11-30, Col. 1:14-16, 1Jh. 5:7-11, Mat. 28:19

Just as God the Father is God, so also Jesus His Son is God in human form.
Phil. 2:3-11, Heb. 5:4-10, Col. 1:14-16. Just as the Father has the power to raise the dead, so does His Son Jh. 5:17-30, 11:1-45, Lk. 7:11-17. LESSON ON WATER BAPTISM

Lk 3:5-18, Matt 3:1-17, Rom 6:1-10, Gal 3:27. 

Memory Verse: Mk 16:15-16 

The doctrine of water baptism is an ordinance commanded by Christ for everyone who has repented from sin. It serves as an outward sign confirming one's confession of faith in Jesus Christ through the gospel, demonstrating wholehearted agreement to die to the old sinful life and to be raised with Christ in the end times (Rom 6:1-10, 1 Cor 15:35-38). 

It is a requisite doctrine that every reborn believer in Christ must undergo to confirm their confession of faith (Acts 3:19-20, Matt 28:19-20, Mk 16:15-20). Many were baptized as infants when they were eight days old or older—what is known as Infant Baptism—which does not align with Scripture and stands contrary to the teachings of Christ (Col 2:11-13). 


THINGS WE MUST KNOW ABOUT WATER BAPTISM
i. It does not wash away sins, nor does it save: Salvation comes solely by grace (Eph 1:7, 2:8-9, Jn 1:12, 1 Pet 3:21-22, 1:18-23). 

ii. It fulfills all righteousness before God: It serves as a witness for Christ, following His own example (Matt 3:13-17). 

iii. It demonstrates faith in the Gospel: Baptism signifies our identification with the death, burial, and resurrection of Jesus (Col 2:11-13, Rom 6:1-10). 

iv. It requires full immersion in water: Baptism is not sprinkling water or making the sign of the cross on a person's forehead; it means fully immersing a person under water (Jn 3:22-23, Acts 8:36-39). 

v. It must be administered in the Trinitarian formula: It must be performed in the name of the Father, and of the Son, and of the Holy Spirit (Matt 28:19). 

vi. It strictly requires prior repentance and faith: Throughout the New Testament, no one was ever baptized prior to receiving salvation. We see clear evidence of genuine confession of sins before baptism, as well as instances where people received the Holy Spirit and prophesied as proof of their redemption before water baptism was administered (Matt 3:4-6, Acts 8:26-39, Acts 8:7-12, Acts 10:44-48). 

vii. There is no scriptural support for Infant Baptism: There is no biblical confirmation or endorsement anywhere in Holy Scripture for infant baptism. 
viii. Qualifications of the Administrator: Only a true minister of God living a holy life—whether an Evangelist, Prophet, or Teacher—is authorized to perform acceptable water baptism (Acts 8:12, Matt 7:15-23).

GENTLENESS: THE FIFTH FRUIT OF THE SPIRIT

Gal 5:22

Gentleness is an attribute of God the Creator, which He has used in dealing with humanity from the fall of man in Eden up to this present day. Were it not for God's gentleness, all living creatures would have been destroyed due to human actions and conduct contrary to God's law and nature (2 Pet 3:3-10, Gen 8:20-21, Gen 6:3-6, Exod 34:6-8, Matt 11:28-30). Gentleness is a fruit that every leader must possess; otherwise, they will fall and bring judgment upon themselves through bad character, just as seen in the leaders of Israel (1 Kings 12:3-4, 14-16, 2 Tim 2:23-26, Num 12:1-11, Mark 10:44-45, 2 Pet 2:4-9, 1 Cor 11:1-2, 1 Pet 5:1-11, Tit 1:7-11, 1 Pet 5:2-3).

Gentleness is an attribute that all followers of Christ should desire so that they may partake in God’s divine nature (1 Pet 2:12, 2 Pet 1:4, 1 Pet 1:15-16, Heb 12:10, Phil 2:15, John 13:13-17). If a believer lacks gentleness, they cannot bear good fruit for God. If a mother lacks gentleness, she will cause her children distress (2 Pet 1:5-11, John 15:1-7, 16, Phil 2:13-15). If the fruit of gentleness is lost between husband and wife, they cannot live in harmony, their prayers will be hindered, and they will be unable to manage their home according to the Word of God (1 Pet 3:1-8, 10, 4:8, Rom 15:1-6).

If gentleness is missing in the life of a minister of God, they cannot be a good steward (1 Pet 5:1-4, Jer 23:1-4, 1 Tim 3:1-7, 2 Tim 2:23-26). It was gentleness that enabled the Lord Jesus to pray for forgiveness for those who nailed Him to the cross. The Lord Jesus used the fruit of gentleness to rescue the Samaritan woman from the well of sin, transforming her into a source of blessing for the whole city (John 4:9-15, 25-26, 28-30, 39-41). Jesus used this same fruit of the Spirit to deliver many sinners during His first earthly ministry (John 8:1-11, Luke 19:1-10, John 5:5-11, Luke 8:37-40).

Jesus used this fruit of gentleness to restore His disciples after their fall (John 20:25-29, 21:4-7, 12-17). It was this fruit that Moses used to deliver Israel from the fiery wrath of God when they turned to idolatry in the wilderness (Exod 32:9-14). This fruit of the Spirit helped Paul convert many Gentiles (Acts 17:19-34, 18:1-4, 2 Cor 10:1, 11, 11:23-26). Paul used this fruit of the Spirit to deliver the church of Galatia from those who brought a false gospel to them (Gal 1:6-8, 4:19-20, 6:1-2).

In conclusion, remember that the opposite of gentleness is violence and ancient corruption, which Holy Scripture commands that we must not mention among holy people (Eph 4:17-24, 5:1-6, 1 Thess 4:1-8).

TEMPERANCE / SELF-CONTROL: THE NINTH FRUIT OF THE SPIRIT


Gal 5:22-23; Job 31:1; Ps 37:1-5, 34, 12-13; Prov 16:32, 25:28; 1 Thess 4:1-8 

As its name implies, temperance means bringing ourselves into subjection under the control of the Spirit of God that dwells within the believer. If a believer does not exercise self-control, they will fall from whatever state they are in, for the Lord Jesus said: "If anyone desires to come after Me, let him deny himself, take up his cross, and follow Me." It prevents a believer from sinning or acting in disobedience to God's guidance. Temperance is the secret to the success of all early believers, both young and old. Conversely, the failure to exercise self-control has brought fall, stumbling, and reproach upon many predecessors (1 Cor 9:24-27, Phil 3:7-19, Gal 2:18-20). It requires determination on the part of a believer to run the race to the very end. Thus, self-control is essential (1 Thess 4:1-8). 


WAYS IN WHICH TEMPERANCE / SELF-CONTROL IS ESSENTIAL:

In shared living spaces, at the workplace, among the public, in the marketplace, on the highway, among unbelievers, and among fellow Christians, there is hardly any area where we should not exercise this fruit of the Spirit: 


•	Control of the eyes: Job 31:1 

•	Control of speech and restraint in listening: Illustration—a commercial vehicle carrying many passengers was moving along, unaware that an army officer in plain clothes, unrecognized by anyone, was on board. As they passed a law enforcement checkpoint, everyone spoke ill of the officers and criticized the money being collected from the driver. However, a believer who exercised self-control preached to the passengers and prayed with sacrificial love among them. As soon as they reached their destination city, the army officer ordered the driver to enter the police station with all passengers, but ordered that particular believer to go in peace. Thus, the believer was delivered from all the harsh words spoken by the others (Jas 1:26; Col 4:6; 1 Pet 4:15-17). 

•	Control over a spirit of anger and wrath: Crucial in society, in the home, between husband and wife, and at the workplace (Gal 2:20; Isa 6:5-7; Prov 25:28, 16:32). 

•	Control regarding food at feasts and general gatherings: Dan 1:8-10; Esth 1:10-20. 

•	Control regarding apparel and personal adornment: 1 Tim 2:9-10; 1 Jn 2:15-17; 1 Pet 3:3-6. 

The Lord Jesus declared concerning our words that by your words you will be justified, and by your words you will be condemned (Matt 12:37). 




SOUL WINNING AMONG THE NEEDY (POOR)


Isa 61:1-2, 1 Tim 2:1-4, John 3:16, Mark 16:15 

The work of winning the souls of the poor and needy is something the Lord Jesus holds in high regard, and He has great compassion for them (Matt 9:35-38, Luke 4:18-21). The vision of winning the souls of humanity begins with the needy and poor, extending all the way to the rich and influential in society. 


WHY WE MUST WIN THE SOULS OF THE NEEDY:


1.	The poor outnumber the rich in society: If we do not win their souls, criminality will continue to rise in society (Rom 10:13-14, Gen 6:5-7, 18:26-32). 

2.	Poverty and lack are breeding grounds for premature death, suicide, sickness, epidemics, fraud, lies, theft, and backsliding: These send multitudes to hell (1 Cor 6:9-11, Ps 9:17). 

3.	Poverty and lack lock away glory, good visions, and precious treasures in bondage (Judg 6:12-16). 

4.	Poverty and lack make people easy prey for false prophets, herbalists, and deceivers: It causes people to believe anything (2 Pet 2:1-3, 14-16). 

5.	Poverty and lack cause people to suffer greatly at the hands of the wealthy and prominent (Gen 26:19-22, 30:27-36, 31:36-42, 39:17-20, 2 Kings 4:1-2). 

6.	Poverty is an instrument used by Satan to afflict people: It makes them doubt the existence, power, and salvation of God (Judg 6:12-13, 2 Kings 7:1-2). 

THE PRECIOUS AND INVALUABLE TREASURE HIDDEN AMONG THE NEEDY:

1 Cor 1:26-31 

The poor and needy fill society. They perform the essential work that keeps our communities beautiful and functional; they handle sanitation tasks that prevent pestilence and widespread death; they use their wisdom and physical strength to farm and provide food for society in abundance; they work various trades such as building houses and constructing roads. These include workers like carpenters, bricklayers, street sweepers, truck drivers, motorcycle riders, fishermen, woodcarvers, and many others. 

WAYS TO WIN THE SOULS OF THE NEEDY AND POOR:



•	In the spirit of compassion and love, just like our Lord Jesus (Matt 9:35-36, 10:7-8, Luke 19:1-10). 

•	Being ready to provide for their needs (Mark 8:1-8, 32-37, Matt 15:29-37, John 21:3-13). 

•	Showing them the way of salvation (Acts 8:5-8, 34-38, Matt 28:19-20). 

WAYS WE CAN MAKE THE FRUIT OF SOUL-WINNING WORK ENDURE:


•	Opening their eyes to the precious treasure within them (Judg 6:12-14). 

•	Supporting them with the Word of God to make it useful (Judg 6:21-29). 

•	Teaching them sound Bible doctrines so they can grow in the Word of God (Luke 11:1-2, Mark 8:31, John 16:12-13). 

•	Giving room/opportunity for questions (Matt 24:3, 16:13-19). 

SOUL WINNING AMONG PROFESSIONALS AND PROMINENT PEOPLE IN SOCIETY


Matt 16:15-20, Acts 5:34-40, 10:22-24 

Professionals and prominent figures in society include individuals like engineers, medical doctors, pharmacists, lawyers, accountants, lecturers, teachers, traders, and business executives. These individuals require special care and attention in the soul-winning ministry of God's church. 


THE IMPORTANCE OF WINNING PROFESSIONALS AND PROMINENT FIGURES IN MINISTRY:

Acts 5:34-40, Acts 22:1-21 

No matter how numerous people are in ministry, if specialized and prominent individuals are lacking, it can cause significant setbacks to the progress of the work. The early church faced heavy persecution from opponents, but when a prominent figure like Gamaliel stood up to speak, they listened to his counsel immediately. 


•	Moses: Educated in deep Egyptian learning under Pharaoh's household, he was used by God to deliver the nation of Israel. 

•	Luke: A medical doctor who wrote the Gospel of Luke and the Acts of the Apostles to Theophilus, his spiritual child, to guard him against false doctrines and ground him in the Lord. This same Luke assisted Peter by writing portions of Peter's letters because Peter was unlearned (illiterate). 

•	Joseph of Arimathea: When Jesus died and the disciples could not stand up to face the government to claim His body, Joseph of Arimathea—a wealthy man of prominent position who was a secret disciple—boldly went to request the body of Jesus. 

•	Paul (Saul of Tarsus): The early church faced intense persecution from Saul of Tarsus, who possessed deep knowledge of the Law under Gamaliel's instruction and management. Jesus revealed Himself to him on the road to Damascus for his salvation and to call him to the Gentiles because of his understanding. Once saved, Paul performed mightier works than many who were before him in the faith due to his level of understanding. Where Peter could not reach, Paul stepped in to go, and God continues to use him through the fourteen epistles he wrote to teach doctrines down to this present day (Acts 9:1-6, 13-22, 26-31). 

WAYS TO WIN PROMINENT PEOPLE AND EXECUTIVES INTO THE CHURCH:



1.	Through peer-to-peer evangelism: Everyone in the church who is already redeemed should reach out to their peers through soul winning, one-on-one (Gen 2:1-2). 

2.	Through friendly visitations: Friendly, personal visits (Luke 19:2-6). 

3.	Through digital outreach: Sending text messages and sharing audio/recorded sermons sent from their friends in church (Acts 1:1, Luke 1:1-4). 

4.	Through testimonies: Sharing and speaking about the testimonies of what God is doing for them (Luke 8:35-40, 2 Kings 5:1-4). 

5.	Through formal invitations: Sending written invitation letters or cards inviting them to church retreats, seminars, and prayer meetings (Acts 8:30-35). 

6.	Through engaging discussions: Engaging in thoughtful dialogue with them so their spiritual eyes may be opened to know the truth, just as the Lord Jesus did initially (John 8:29-32, Luke 2:43-47). 

SOME PROMINENT FIGURES WHO WERE USEFUL TO GOD IN THEIR TIME:


•	Joseph: Used God's wisdom to store food for seven years to save lives (Gen 45:4-8). 

•	Moses: Used the privilege of his Egyptian education for God's glory (Heb 11:24-27). 

•	Luke (the Doctor): Was useful in Paul's ministry alongside Demas the lawyer. 

•	Key Historical Figures: Figures like Martin Luther (lawyer), John Wesley (founder of the Holy Methodist Church), Jonathan Edwards, and Charles G. Finney (great revivalist) embraced the vision of soul winning, utilizing their understanding and knowledge to spread the light of the gospel in their time. 
SOUL WINNING AMONG YOUTHS

The commandment given by Jesus to His disciples does not exclude young people, for the youth are tomorrow’s church and tomorrow’s ministers of God. Failing to win the youths in our society surrenders them to widespread criminality, which leads to untimely death, sickness, foolishness, insanity, cruelty, and love of money (Rom 10:13-14).


THE HIDDEN VALUE AND TREASURE IN YOUTHS:
1 John 2:14

Youths possess strength, wisdom, boldness, and the capacity to ground God's Word in their hearts. They do not fear, they have courage, they can defend society against battle, they are advocates, and they are effective for proclamation.

It is these very qualities that Satan exploits in youths who stray from God's way, initiating them into various secret societies, promoting idolatry, and placing them on frontlines to defend nations or serve politicians in corrupt ways, as well as using them in sports and entertainment. Just as Satan desires to use youths, God also desires to use them for His work.

The Lord Jesus used youths in His ministry because He recognized the value and treasure hidden within them. Examples of young people who were useful for the Gospel include:


•	Josiah, Joseph, Ruth, Esther, John, Timothy, and the four virgin daughters of Philip (2 Tim 3:14-15, 4:11, Acts 9:13-16).

Youths are the pillars of the church. We must not despise or look down on young people in the church; if we reach them with the Gospel early, they will stand firmly by their convictions without fear of death on the foundation laid before them (Deut 6:6-7, Eccl 12:1, Mark 16:15-20).


WAYS TO WIN THE SOULS OF YOUTHS (Matt 28:19-20):
•	Through Teaching and Guidance: Youths love learning; Jesus said we should teach them (Josh 1:8). We must present the way of salvation to them along with its benefits (Ps 91:1-16, Gen 12:1-3, John 1:11-12).

•	Through Peer Evangelism: Sending their saved peers to reach out to them two by two (Matt 10:5-8).

•	Through Invitations and Testimonies: Extending invitations to open-air revivals, camps, and prayer meetings, accompanied by testimonies of God’s miraculous works (John 4:28-30).

•	Through Church Educational Centers: Establishing educational/literacy centers where youths gather to learn.
...offering free training programs and holiday coaching classes, using those opportunities to share the Word of God with them (Acts 18:7-11). 


•	Through Campus Fellowships: Establishing campus fellowships led by qualified and spirit-filled ministers of God to address the needs of students in higher institutions of learning. 

•	Through Vibrant Youth Camps: Organizing lively youth camps equipped with attractive, modern programs and activities relevant to contemporary trends (Acts 17:10-32). 

Paul the Apostle stands as an exemplary model of soul winning among students in his generation. He utilized his education, understanding, and gifts for the advancement of the Lord's work; even though he joined the church later, he labored tirelessly in the ministry (1 Cor 15:3-11). 

SOUL WINNING AMONG CHILDREN

Ps 51:5 

Soul winning among children is something rare among the children of God today. Many ministers of God pay no attention to children, which is why out of five to ten children, it is hard to find genuine salvation among them in the church today. Yet Jesus said it is not the will of God that one of these little ones should perish (Matt 18:14, 19:14). 

If the church fails to win the souls of children, it poses a great danger for tomorrow. Many beautiful places of worship will be sold to become event centers, tourist centers, or various commercial shops when these children grow up with the mindset that place of worship is meant only for the elderly (Rom 10:14). 


THE HIDDEN TREASURES IN CHILDREN:
They have tender hearts and absorb things quickly. They do not possess a heart of hatred, rebellion, or division; they have a spirit of fellowship, forgive one another easily, fear easily, love to learn, and have sharp minds. If they are reached early with the Word of God, they never forget (Matt 18:1-4, Prov 22:6). A child easily spreads news among peers and is useful for publicity. Many nursery school founders leverage them to announce programs in unity during morning devotions, marching into class together while chanting memory verses in unison. When passing parents hear them, it attracts them to enroll their own children as well. 

If we do not win their souls for Jesus early, a child quickly becomes corrupt. Satan will exploit the precious potential within them, luring them into secret cults, witchcraft, sorcery, and darkness using food provisions disguised under birthday celebrations. 

Much of children's glory has been stolen due to their lack of spiritual understanding. Many who seek money rituals target children through various means, such as immorality, deception, stealing their virtues, or seeking their blood for ritual sacrifices. 

Parents who focus solely on academic education while neglecting to instil the Word of God in their children are in great danger. Therefore, soul winning for children must begin right from the home (Deut 6:6-9, 20-24). 


WAYS TO WIN THE SOULS OF CHILDREN (Prov 22:6):



•	Helping them understand the foundational truth of the Word of God: We must teach them that everyone is born in sin (Ps 51:5) and every unrepentant sinner will perish forever in the lake of fire (Ps 9:17, Rev 20:15). 

•	Explaining true repentance: Showing them what true repentance means as set forth in God's Word (Isa 55:6-7, Acts 3:19-20, Jonah 3:1-10, 1 John 1:6-8). 

•	Presenting Jesus as the only Savior: Showing them Jesus (John 1:11-12, 1 John 3:1-3), the only Savior who can redeem the world (John 3:16, Acts 4:12). 

•	Using real-life stories and color symbolism: Telling true stories to reinforce our teaching and using colors to illustrate concepts—such as Black for a sinful/dirty heart, White for a clean/pure heart, and Red for the blood of Jesus that washes away sins, and so forth. 

•	Using drama, visual aids, and media: Drama plays help them grasp salvation quickly. Showing pictures and films like the Jesus Film, as well as Bible stories teaching how God created the world—pointing out the sky, sun, moon, mountains, and valleys surrounding us—demonstrates the work and existence of God. 

•	Teaching the reality of Heaven and Hell: Clearly explaining the truth about Heaven and Hell and the kind of people who will go to each place. 

EXAMPLES OF CHILDREN WHO WERE SAVED:


•	Miss Phebe Bartlett: Saved at just four years old. 

•	Jonathan Edwards: Saved at eight years old and became a great preacher. 

•	Matthew Henry: Saved at eleven years old. 

•	Adam Clarke: Saved at fourteen years old; both he and Matthew Henry grew in Christ to become authors of renowned Bible Commentaries. 

Just as Josiah (who began to reign at eight years old), Samuel, David, Jeremiah, and Timothy were used by God from their youth, we must not leave children vulnerable during dangerous times for the enemy to exploit.

GODLY SORROW

2Cor. 7:8-10; Psalm 51:17, 137:1-6, 18:4-20 

According to this topic, which means godly sorrow, it is the state of our heart because of a specific need that we face at a specific time, which can cause the heart to be wounded or lifted up to embrace Fasting or prayer for a turn-around from such a state. Various names can be used to describe godly sorrow, such as lowliness of heart, a broken heart, accepting guilt, poor in spirit, a sorrowful heart, and so forth, which points to the same responsibility before God from inside our heart. Man looks at the outward appearance, but God looks at the kind of heart we have when we long for anything before He answers. 

How godly sorrow comes. This can happen when a person hears the word of God concerning his need, or it can happen because of the condition of one's life, family, relative, nation, or church of God. This kind of heart sends confession of sin and repentance to Him. It is in this kind of heart that we push God for help, mercy, remembrance, redemption, healing, and various miracles; such a heart longs for Gen. 15:1-6; Judg. 6:12-14; Ex. 3:7-10. No one can receive salvation, healing, restoration, and various living blessings from God without this kind of heart Isa. 66:2; Psalm 51:17. 

Godly sorrow comes through:


1.	Hearing the clear word of truth that reveals a state of need along with rebuke concerning our call and errors Psalm 139:1-12; 2 Cor. 7:8-10; 2 Kings 22:11-19. 

2.	Hearing words of reproach that insult our faith 1 Sam. 1:6-18; Isa. 37:11-20; Psalm 42:1-4; Neh. 1:1-7. 

3.	The condition of the church, nation, or family of a person 1 Sam. 1:6-10; Jon. 3:1-10; Judg. 6:12-14; Acts 4:23-33. 

4.	It can be through hearing news of coming war, terrifying dreams, evil prophecies, visions, and revelations that cause despair 2 Kings 20:1-5; Isa. 6:1-7; Gen. 32:6-13, 24-28; Isa. 37:11-20; 2Sam. 12:7-17. 

5.	It can come through conviction in our heart because of dishonesty, unjust conduct, and deceitful behavior 
to a specific person Lk. 19:5-9, 23:39-43, 18:10-14; Mk. 7:25-30, Lk. 15:17-24. 

If this kind of heart is lost in the life of a messenger of God, his ministry will not bear fruit. His preaching will become like empty loud noise, he will become a comedian in the eyes of the church members, signs and wonders will cease, and holy living will become impossible among the flock Psalm 50:16-22; Mat. 7:15-20, 23:13-15. Mountain prayer is a method by which a believer separates themselves to turn away from worldly distractions to face the Lord for important matters—whether an individual needs to receive an answer to prayer, fight back against an enemy, receive God’s righteous laws, open up to people, or remind God of His covenant. 

Mountain prayer helps rescue a believer from the hands of the wicked and from the trap of sin. It helps bring a person’s heart close to God in perfection without obstruction, and blocks out the noise of sin so that one can clearly hear the voice of God. A mountain is also a place where we go to receive fresh power and the inspiration of God. It is a place where we climb high into the realm of prayer when we face life's battles or look for alternative paths (Habakkuk 3:1–2; Joel 1:13–20, 2:12–28; 2 Chronicles 7:14; Isaiah 65:21–24). 

While mountain prayer is very beneficial, it is also dangerous—especially for a beginner believer or someone who does not have the Spirit of God. This is because many temptations can cause a believer to enter the traps of false prophets, false teachings, false prophecies, and false visions, which bring division into a joyful family. Believing false visions or having others manipulate you through strange and traditional ways is a common trap. Because of this, God warned Israel to be careful about where they offered sacrifices to Him (Deuteronomy 12:13–15, 30–32, 13:1–6; Matthew 7:15–20).
