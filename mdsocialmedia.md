I am deeply humbled and honored to receive the Special Recognition Award for Distinguished Service to Literature from the Association of Nigerian Authors, presented during their 45th International Convention.

Looking back on my journey across the publishing and educational landscape, this honor serves as a powerful reminder of why we do what we do. Literature is far more than words on a page. It is the repository of our cultural identity, the bedrock of education, and the primary vehicle through which future generations learn to dream, think, and lead.

Throughout my tenure leading industry bodies like the Nigerian Publishers Association and the Nigerian Book Fair Trust, as well as serving on the board of the Nigerian Educational Research and Development Council and the executive committee of the International Publishers Association, my goal has always remained singular. That goal is to build an ecosystem where Nigerian authors thrive, educational content remains world class, and access to knowledge is seamless for every reader.

Receiving this award from the authors themselves makes it especially meaningful. Publishers and writers share a unique partnership. One cannot flourish without the other, and together we carry the responsibility of shaping minds and preserving our nation's stories.

I extend my sincere gratitude to the leadership, trustees, and members of the Association of Nigerian Authors for this thoughtful gesture. I also want to thank my dedicated colleagues, partners, and family whose unwavering support makes this work possible.

This recognition is not a destination, but a call to keep working, innovating, and advocating for the growth of Nigerian literature and digital educational solutions across our continent.

I recently had the privilege of participating in the Sharjah International Book Fair – NYU School of Professional Studies Center for Publishing, Writing, and Media Publishing Training Session 2026.

In publishing, as in any industry that shapes how people learn and grow, the work never stands still. Markets shift. Technology advances. Readers' expectations evolve. Remaining effective means remaining open — to new ideas, global conversations, and better ways of serving the ecosystems we are part of.

This year's engagement in Sharjah was a timely opportunity to reflect, exchange perspectives, and deepen thinking around the future of publishing. As Accessible Publishers Limited approaches three decades of contribution to education and literature in Nigeria, moments like these remind me that longevity is sustained not by past milestones, but by a willingness to keep learning and adapting.

My appreciation goes to the Sharjah International Book Fair, the Sharjah Book Authority, and the NYU School of Professional Studies Center for Publishing, Writing, and Media for creating a space where practitioners from around the world can think together about what comes next.

I look forward to applying these insights as we continue building stronger pathways for authors, educators, and learners across our continent.
