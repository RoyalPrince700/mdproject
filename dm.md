## Class 1 — Introduction to Digital Marketing

**Purpose of this class:** Establish the difference between traditional and digital marketing, map the main digital channels, introduce SEO, CRO, RACE, and SOSTAC, and set a repeatable method for planning campaigns.

**By the end of Class 1 you should be able to:**

- Explain how traditional and digital marketing work together, not against each other.
- Choose channels using audience, budget, and campaign objective.
- Diagnose a weak campaign with CRO instead of only rewriting the ad.
- Plan day-to-day ads with **RACE** and present to leadership with **SOSTAC**.
- Build a campaign from the problem the product solves through to top-of-mind awareness.

---

### 1. Traditional marketing and digital marketing

**Traditional marketing** uses offline, physical channels to reach people where they live, work, and move. Typical examples include billboards and other outdoor signage, lamp-post banners, flyers, posters, radio, television, print, and event activations. These channels are strong for mass awareness and local presence.

**Digital marketing (DM)** uses online channels — search, social media, email, websites, apps, and paid digital media — to reach, engage, convert, and retain audiences. Digital channels are strong for targeting, measurement, and moving people through a defined customer journey.

**They are not rivals.** A strong campaign often combines both, because each family of channels does a different job:

- A **fintech launch** (for example Smipay) might use billboards and lamp-post banners for mass awareness, then Meta or Google ads for targeted app downloads and sign-ups.
- A **school platform** (for example SmartEdu Hub) might use radio on Oxygen FM Ibadan for broad local reach, then Instagram and Facebook ads aimed at SS2–SS3 students and parents.
- **Accessible Publishers** might use print and school-fair presence, then content marketing and SEO to send interested schools to SmartEdu Hub.

**Tutor rule:** Choose channels because they fit the audience, the budget, and the campaign objective — not because one type of marketing is automatically “better” than the other.

### 2. What digital marketing includes

Digital marketing is a mix of paid, owned, and earned activity. Class 1 emphasised the channels below and the budget logic that should guide them.

| Area | Examples | Tutor guidance |
|------|----------|----------------|
| **SEO** | Google, Bing | On-page and off-page work that improves organic (unpaid) search visibility |
| **Paid search and display** | Google Ads | Among the most expensive channels, especially for competitive keywords. Use mainly when the budget is high |
| **Social ads** | Meta (Facebook and Instagram) | More affordable. The tutor’s default when the budget is moderate or tight |
| **Professional ads** | LinkedIn Ads | Also expensive. Use when a B2B or professional audience justifies the cost |
| **Short-form video** | TikTok Ads | Growing channel. Strong for younger and student audiences |
| **X (formerly Twitter)** | Often via partner networks | X commonly works through partner ad networks and agencies. Google can serve ads on X in some setups |
| **Content marketing** | Blogs, guides, case studies, video, newsletters | Builds trust and supports SEO over time |
| **Influencer marketing** | Educators, finance creators, campus voices, radio personalities | Reach and credibility through people the audience already trusts |

**Remember:** Channel choice is a budget decision as well as a creative decision. Do not default to Google Ads or LinkedIn Ads on a tight budget.

### 3. Search Engine Optimisation (SEO)

SEO is the practice of improving visibility in **organic** (unpaid) search results. It is a long-term channel: it supports awareness, credibility, and lower-cost traffic over time, and it makes paid campaigns more efficient.

**Search engines to optimise for:**

- **Google** — dominant in most markets, including Nigeria.
- **Bing (Microsoft)** — secondary, but still worth including. Some corporate and desktop users rely on it.

**Core SEO elements emphasised in Class 1:**

- **On-page SEO** — titles, headings, keyword use, page speed, mobile experience, and a clear site structure.
- **Off-page SEO** — activity outside your own website that builds reputation, referral traffic, and authority.
- **Backlinks** — links from other reputable sites to yours. Search engines treat these as signals of trust.
- **Blogs and content** — regular, useful articles that answer what the audience actually searches for. Good content supports keywords and earns backlinks.

**Off-page SEO for Nigerian tech and fintech products:**

When the product is fintech or technology (for example **Smipay**), industry media coverage is part of off-page SEO, not a separate “nice to have.” Priority publications from class:

- **TechCabal**
- **TechPoint**
- **NairaMetrics**

A feature or review on these platforms can deliver referral traffic, brand credibility, and valuable backlinks. Pair that coverage with a strong landing page and, later, retargeting for readers who do not convert on the first visit.

### 4. Content marketing and influencer marketing

**Content marketing** means creating useful material — blog posts, explainers, case studies, short videos, and newsletters — that educates or helps the audience **before** you ask them to buy or sign up. Over time, content builds trust, improves SEO, and supports every stage of the customer journey. It is a long-term investment that makes paid ads work harder.

Examples for this group:

- “How schools use an SMS to track fees and results” (SmartEdu Hub).
- “Five ways PQ exam prep helps SS3 students” (SmartEdu Hub / exam bodies).
- A payment-security explainer for first-time wallet users (Smipay).

**Influencer marketing** means partnering with people the audience already trusts — educators, finance creators, campus voices, and radio personalities — to recommend or demonstrate the product. This is especially useful in Nigeria, where social proof and trusted voices often accelerate adoption.

Examples:

- A teacher showing how PQ exam prep works on the SmartEdu Hub LMS.
- A parent-focused creator explaining how the SMS helps track school fees and results.
- A finance creator walking through a Smipay payment.

### 5. Conversion Rate Optimisation (CRO)

When an ad is running but results are weak — clicks without sign-ups, high spend with few conversions — do **not** only change the creative. Diagnose the full path from click to completed action. That discipline is **CRO**.

| Check | Question to ask |
|-------|-----------------|
| **Link** | Is the destination URL correct and working on mobile and desktop? |
| **Speed** | Is the website or landing page slow, especially on mobile networks? |
| **Landing page** | Does the page match the ad message? Is the call-to-action (CTA) clear? |
| **Strategy** | Are we targeting the wrong audience, the wrong objective, or the wrong channel? |
| **Offer** | Is the value proposition weak, unclear, or uncompetitive? |

CRO is the work of finding and fixing friction so more visitors complete the desired action. Traffic that does not convert is wasted budget.

---

### 6. The purpose of digital marketing — beyond traffic

Digital marketing is **not only about driving traffic**.

The tutor’s framing: **DM moves people through a journey that has an end goal.**

Typical journey:

1. **Awareness** — they learn that the product or campaign exists.
2. **Interest / action** — they click, register, download, or enquire.
3. **Conversion** — they pay, subscribe, onboard, or become a customer.
4. **Engagement / retention** — they return, keep using the product, and refer others.

Every campaign should define that end goal first, then choose channels and messages for each stage. Traffic without a destination wastes money.

### 7. RACE framework (day-to-day campaign stack)

**RACE** is the framework the tutor uses most often because it is practical for running ads.

| Letter | Stage | Purpose |
|--------|-------|---------|
| **R** | **Reach** | Create awareness. Let people know the product or campaign exists. |
| **A** | **Act** | Prompt a useful action — register, click, sign up, enquire, or download. |
| **C** | **Conversion** | Turn interest into a measurable outcome — purchase, subscription, completed lead, or school onboarding. |
| **E** | **Engage** | Retain and re-engage converted users so they keep visiting, using, and recommending the product. |

**How the tutor structures ad sets**

**When the budget is high — four ad sets:**

1. **Reach** — awareness-only creative.
2. **Act** — action-focused creative (register, sign up, enquire).
3. **Conversion** — optimise for people who complete the main goal.
4. **Engage** — retention, reminders, upsell, and community.

**When the budget is tight — three ad sets:**

1. **Reach + Act combined** — one set of creative that introduces the product **and** invites immediate action.
2. **Conversion**
3. **Engage**

This keeps funnel logic without spreading a small budget across too many ad sets.

### 8. SOSTAC framework (for MD and executive presentations)

Use **SOSTAC** when presenting to the Managing Director or senior leadership. It answers what executives need to see: context, goals, plan, execution, and measurement.

| Letter | Stage | What it covers |
|--------|-------|----------------|
| **S** | **Situation analysis** | Where we are now — market, competitors, current performance, SWOT, audience insights, and constraints. |
| **O** | **Objectives** | What the campaign must achieve — SMART goals tied to the problem the product solves. |
| **S** | **Strategy** | High-level approach — which segments, channels, positioning, and customer journey we will use. |
| **T** | **Tactics** | Specific tools and activities — Meta ads, Google, influencers, SEO, email, landing pages, radio. |
| **A** | **Action** | Who does what, timeline, budget allocation, creative production, and launch checklist. |
| **C** | **Control** | KPIs, reporting cadence, optimisation rules, CRO checks, and retargeting triggers. |

**RACE versus SOSTAC**

- **RACE** — planning and running campaigns day to day.
- **SOSTAC** — formal proposals, board reviews, and MD-level presentations.

In practice: **plan and run with RACE; document and present with SOSTAC.**

### 9. Retargeting

After roughly **two months** of running awareness and conversion campaigns, start **retargeting** — showing ads again to people who have already interacted. That includes people who visited the site, watched a video, engaged with an ad, or started a sign-up but did not finish.

They already know the brand, so cost per conversion is often lower than for cold audiences. Retargeting supports **Conversion** and **Engage** in RACE.

Example: show SmartEdu Hub PQ prep ads to people who watched most of an awareness video but have not registered.

### 10. Campaign thought process

When planning a campaign for any brand in the group, follow this sequence. Do not start with “we have an app” or “we should run ads.”

#### Step 1 — Know the problem the product solves

State the customer problem in plain language. SmartEdu Hub, for example, solves fragmented school administration, weak learning delivery, and exam-preparation gaps — not “we have a platform.”

#### Step 2 — Identify the target audience

Who must change behaviour? Students, parents, school owners, bursars, teachers, listeners, or merchants — it depends on the offer.

#### Step 3 — Build buyer personas

A **buyer persona** is a detailed profile of a segment you will speak to with specific messaging and channels. Create as many personas as the product line needs (LMS, SMS, PQ exam bodies, Smipay wallets, Oxygen FM listeners, and so on).

**SmartEdu Hub examples from class:**

| Persona | Profile | Likely channel |
|---------|---------|----------------|
| **Persona 1 — Student** | Age 15 and above, SS2–SS3, exam-focused, mobile-first | Meta (Instagram and Facebook) |
| **Persona 2 — Parent** | Often women with children in secondary school; cares about results and school quality | Meta, WhatsApp groups, radio |
| **Persona 3 — School admin** | Proprietor, principal, or bursar — needs SMS, fees, and reports | LinkedIn, email, direct sales, events |

#### Step 4 — Competitor audit

Ask: What do competitors have that we do not? What are they doing better? If a competitor posts six times a day and we post twice, we may need more frequency or stronger content — not a blind copy of their work, but a plan to close the visibility gap.

#### Step 5 — Demographics

Age, gender, location, income, device use, and language must match the persona and the channel. A mismatch here wastes spend.

#### Step 6 — Top-of-mind awareness

**Top-of-mind awareness** means that when someone thinks of the category, your brand comes to mind first. It is built by consistent presence across channels — ads, content, influencers, radio, PR, and retargeting — so the audience keeps seeing you.

For the Accessible group, a parent might hear Oxygen FM, see a SmartEdu Hub Instagram ad, read a TechCabal story about Smipay, and later be retargeted. Those touches should reinforce one ecosystem, not five disconnected brands.

|---------|-----------------|

### 12. Class 1 key takeaways

1. **DM means Digital Marketing** in every context in this course.
2. Traditional and digital marketing work best together when audience and budget require both.
3. Google Ads and LinkedIn Ads are expensive; **Meta is the tutor’s affordable default**.
4. SEO covers **Google and Bing**. Off-page work for fintech and tech includes **TechCabal, TechPoint, and NairaMetrics**.
5. When ads underperform, use **CRO** on the full path — link, speed, landing page, strategy, and offer — not only the creative.
6. Digital marketing moves people through a journey: awareness → action → conversion → engagement.
7. Use **RACE** to run campaigns. Use **SOSTAC** to present to the MD.
8. High budget: four RACE ad sets. Tight budget: three (Reach + Act combined, then Conversion, then Engage).
9. Begin retargeting after about **two months** of awareness and conversion work.
10. Campaign planning order: **problem → audience → personas → competitors → demographics → top of mind**.
