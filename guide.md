One-Minute Research Pitch
My dissertation explores how executives in Nigerian book publishing firms perceive and integrate information technology into B2B marketing strategies. The study responds to a practical problem: publishers serve institutional buyers such as schools, libraries, bookstores, distributors, and procurement bodies, but many still rely on traditional relationship-driven marketing while buyers increasingly use digital procurement and communication systems. Using a qualitative multiple case study of approximately six publishing firms, I will collect interviews and two classes of documents to understand not only what technology is being used, but why strategic integration succeeds or fails. The study is guided primarily by TOE, supplemented by relationship marketing theory and institutional theory. Its expected contribution is applied guidance for publishing executives, policymakers, and scholars of digital transformation in emerging economies.
Defense Anchors to Repeat Confidently
This is not a study of technology tools alone; it is a study of strategic integration in a relationship-based B2B industry.
The design is qualitative because the research questions ask how executives understand, explain, and experience IT integration.
The multiple case design strengthens the study by allowing depth within firms and comparison across firms.
The three data sources protect the study from relying only on self-report.
TOE explains adoption conditions; relationship marketing theory explains B2B trust; institutional theory explains external pressures.
The study is bounded, feasible, ethically controlled, and aligned from problem to purpose to research questions to methods.


Chapter One Summary: Introduction
Chapter One establishes the foundation of the study. It explains that information technology has transformed global publishing and B2B marketing, but Nigerian book publishers still rely heavily on personal visits, telephone calls, informal networks, and partial digital visibility. The chapter positions this as a strategic problem because institutional buyers are becoming more digital, while publishers may not be sufficiently integrated into those systems.
Area	Simple but comprehensive explanation
Core issue	Nigerian book publishers increasingly face digital procurement and relationship-management expectations, yet many still depend on personal visits, telephone calls, and informal networks rather than structured digital B2B systems.
Specific problem	Publishing executives have not strategically integrated IT into B2B marketing; this weakens visibility in institutional procurement processes, destabilizes sales pipelines, and limits access to major contracts.
Four problem drivers	Superficial IT use; piracy and weak enforcement; infrastructure deficits such as power, connectivity and data cost; and internal finance and digital-skills constraints.
Purpose	To explore how Nigerian publishing executives perceive the role of IT in B2B marketing strategy and the challenges they experience during integration.
Research questions	RQ1 asks how executives describe the role of IT in B2B marketing strategies. RQ2 asks what challenges executives experience when integrating IT into those strategies.
Framework	TOE is the primary framework; relationship marketing theory explains trust and commitment in B2B exchange; institutional theory explains regulatory, normative and cultural pressures.
Method preview	Qualitative multiple case study using interviews, firm-produced documents, and external procurement/industry records, analyzed through Braun and Clarke thematic analysis.

How to defend Chapter One:
Make the problem practical: lost visibility, weak procurement access, unstable sales pipelines, and reduced competitiveness.
Make the purpose exploratory: you are studying perceptions and challenges, not testing whether IT improves sales.
Make the alignment explicit: problem, purpose, research questions, framework, design, data sources, and analysis all point to the same phenomenon.
Chapter Two Summary: Literature Review
Chapter Two reviews the scholarship that justifies the study and identifies the gap. It demonstrates that publishing is not only an editorial or production activity; in Nigeria it is a relational B2B business where publishers depend on schools, libraries, bookstores, distributors, government agencies, and procurement systems. The literature also shows that digital transformation has strategic potential, but Nigerian evidence is still limited, partial, and often descriptive.
Area	Simple but comprehensive explanation
Literature logic	The review moves from global digital transformation and B2B sales technologies to the distinctive history, relational structure, and constraints of Nigerian book publishing.
Publishing as B2B	Nigerian publishing is shown as a relational industry dominated by schools, libraries, bookstores, distributors, and procurement actors, not simply by individual readers.
Technology context	Digital tools can support CRM, e-commerce, analytics, social media, online procurement engagement, and digital distribution, but Nigerian evidence shows use is often partial or publicity-oriented.
Organizational context	Leadership disposition, financing, internal skills, resource availability, and organizational culture affect whether experimentation becomes strategic integration.
Environmental context	Piracy, weak enforcement, infrastructure gaps, procurement rules, market pressures, and institutional norms shape executive technology choices.
Integrated framework	TOE explains adoption conditions, relationship marketing preserves the relational logic of B2B publishing, and institutional theory specifies the pressures in the external environment.
Gaps	Contextual gap: publishing is not just another SME sector. Methodological gap: surveys cannot explain why. Explanatory gap: TOE interactions are underexplored. Applied gap: little evidence on what firms are actually doing to move from diagnosis to solution.
Contribution	The study contributes a case-based, executive-level explanation of how and why IT shapes B2B marketing in a sector central to education and cultural production in Nigeria.

How to defend Chapter Two:
Show that the literature review is not a list of studies; it is a logic chain from global digital transformation to the Nigerian publishing gap.
Emphasize the four gaps: contextual, methodological, explanatory, and applied knowledge.
Be ready to defend the three-framework integration: TOE alone is strong, but it does not fully explain trust-based B2B relationships or institutional pressures.
Chapter Three Summary: Methodology
Chapter Three presents and defends the methodology. It shows that the research design fits the research questions because the study needs contextual depth, not statistical measurement. The chapter also specifies population, sampling, ethics, instrumentation, data collection, analysis, limitations, delimitations, trustworthiness, and researcher positionality.
Area	Simple but comprehensive explanation
Design	The study is qualitative and uses a multiple case study design because the phenomenon is embedded in real firms and requires contextual, comparative explanation.
Unit of analysis	Each participating publishing firm is a bounded organizational case; executives and documents provide evidence within the case.
Participants	12 to 18 senior executives across approximately six firms, with two to three executives per case. Roles include executive directors, marketing directors, senior sales executives, and digital/IT directors.
Sampling	Purposeful maximum variation sampling across publishing segment, firm size, and level of digital adoption.
Data sources	Semi-structured interviews; 24 to 36 firm-produced marketing/corporate documents; and 18 to 30 external procurement and industry records.
Ethics	No data collection before IRB approval. Consent, site permission, confidentiality, pseudonyms, encrypted storage, 3-year retention, voluntary participation, and safeguards against coercion.
Instrumentation	Researcher-developed interview and document review protocols, anchored in the research questions and frameworks, peer reviewed, pilot tested, and logged in an audit trail.
Analysis	Six-phase thematic analysis in NVivo; within-case analysis first, cross-case synthesis second; inductive coding sensitized by TOE, relationship marketing, and institutional theory.
Trustworthiness	Credibility through triangulation and participant validation; transferability through thick description; dependability through audit trail; confirmability through reflexive journaling, memoing, negative cases, and peer debriefing.

How to defend Chapter Three:
Repeatedly link method to research questions: perceptions, meanings, challenges, and context require qualitative inquiry.
Clarify the unit of analysis: the publishing firm is the case; executives and documents provide evidence within each case.
Defend rigor through triangulation, audit trail, peer review, pilot testing, reflexivity, and trustworthiness criteria.


Likely Questions from the Chair and Strong Responses
Likely question	Scholar-level response
State your study in one minute.	My study examines how Nigerian book publishing executives understand and integrate information technology into B2B marketing strategies. The core concern is not whether technology exists, but why strategic integration remains shallow in an industry whose revenues depend heavily on institutional buyers such as schools, libraries, bookstores, and procurement bodies. I use a qualitative multiple case study across approximately six publishing firms, triangulating executive interviews with firm documents and external procurement or industry records. The study is anchored in TOE, supported by relationship marketing theory and institutional theory.
What exactly is the problem?	The specific problem is that executives of Nigerian book publishing firms have not successfully and strategically integrated IT into B2B marketing. They often use social media, phone calls, WhatsApp, websites, or emails, but not necessarily as a structured system for customer relationship management, procurement engagement, analytics, or account development. This creates weaker visibility in digital procurement processes and unstable sales pipelines.
Why is this problem important enough for DBA research?	It is a business problem with strategic, practical, and societal implications. Strategically, publishers may lose access to major institutional contracts. Practically, firms need evidence to guide technology investment and capability development. Socially, a more efficient publishing sector can improve access to educational materials in Nigeria. The DBA contribution is applied knowledge for decision makers, not purely theoretical abstraction.
What is your purpose statement?	The purpose is to explore how publishing executives of book publishing firms in Nigeria perceive the role of IT in shaping B2B marketing strategies and the challenges they experience when integrating IT into those strategies.
What are your research questions?	RQ1 asks how Nigerian publishing executives describe the role of IT in B2B marketing strategies. RQ2 asks what challenges they experience when integrating IT into B2B marketing strategies. Together, the questions align with the problem, the purpose, the qualitative design, and the TOE-based framework.
Why are there no hypotheses?	The study is qualitative and exploratory. It seeks meanings, perceptions, managerial reasoning, and contextual explanations rather than measurement, prediction, or hypothesis testing. Hypotheses would be more suitable for a quantitative design after the current study has developed richer explanatory propositions.
What is your central argument?	The central argument is that IT integration in Nigerian publishing B2B marketing is not merely a technology issue. It is shaped by the interaction of technological readiness, organizational resources and leadership, relationship-based market routines, and institutional pressures such as piracy, regulation, infrastructure, and procurement practices.
What would make the committee confident that the study is feasible?	The study is feasible because the scope is bounded to Nigeria-based book publishing firms, senior executives with direct responsibility for B2B marketing or IT-enabled sales, and three accessible data sources. The proposed sample size and document volumes are rigorous but manageable within a doctoral timeline, and the design avoids live observation of commercially sensitive negotiations.



Likely Faculty Committee Questions and Scholar-Level Responses
Likely question	Scholar-level response
Why did you choose a qualitative method instead of a quantitative survey?	Because the problem is not simply whether publishers use technology, but how executives make sense of technology, why integration is shallow, and how constraints interact in real organizational contexts. A survey could map adoption rates, but it would not adequately capture the reasoning, sequence, tensions, or contextual trade-offs behind executive decisions.
Why a multiple case study rather than a single case study?	A multiple case study allows depth within each firm and comparison across different publishing contexts. Because firms vary by size, segment, and digital maturity, multiple cases allow replication logic: I can examine whether patterns recur across cases or differ under specific technological, organizational, or environmental conditions.
What is the unit of analysis?	The primary unit of analysis is the publishing firm as a bounded case. Executives provide participant-level accounts, and documents provide supporting evidence, but the analytic claim is about firm-level B2B marketing strategy and IT integration within the Nigerian publishing context.
How will you select cases?	I will use purposeful maximum variation logic. Cases will vary across publishing segment, firm size, and level of digital adoption. This supports richer analysis because the study can compare firms that are mainly traditional with firms using digital tools, CRM, or distribution platforms.
Why is 12 to 18 participants enough?	The number is justified by saturation and information power rather than statistical calculation. The study has a focused purpose, a specific population, and multiple data sources. Two to three executives per case across about six cases should provide sufficient depth, with flexibility to expand modestly if themes remain underdeveloped.
How will you handle bias as an industry insider?	I acknowledge that my experience gives me contextual access and sensitivity, but also creates risk of assumptions. I will mitigate this through reflexive journaling before and after interviews, an audit trail, peer debriefing, careful use of probes rather than leading questions, negative case analysis, and de-identified reporting that foregrounds participants’ evidence rather than my prior views.
Why did you not include direct observation?	Live B2B marketing and procurement interactions may involve commercially sensitive negotiations, third-party clients, confidential pricing, and access challenges. Instead, I use two classes of archival/documentary evidence: firm-produced documents and external procurement or industry records. Comparing those with interview accounts allows me to examine the gap between stated practice and market requirement without creating unnecessary ethical and access risks.
How will you triangulate your data?	Triangulation will occur by comparing three sources within each case: executive interviews, firm-produced marketing or corporate documents, and external procurement or industry records. If executives claim digital maturity, I can compare that claim with the firm’s own materials and external procurement expectations. The same thematic analysis procedure applies across all sources.
Why is TOE the primary framework?	TOE is appropriate because IT integration in firms is shaped by technological, organizational, and environmental contexts. The Nigerian publishing problem reflects all three: available tools and perceived complexity, leadership and resource constraints, and external conditions such as piracy, infrastructure, procurement requirements, and regulation.
Why add relationship marketing theory?	Nigerian book publishing B2B marketing is relational. Institutional sales depend on trust, commitment, repeat orders, and long-term ties with schools, libraries, bookstores, distributors, and procurement actors. Relationship marketing theory helps prevent the study from treating technology as a replacement for relationships; it examines how technology mediates or strengthens them.
Why add institutional theory?	Institutional theory clarifies the environmental pressures that TOE names but does not fully specify. In Nigerian publishing, regulation, industry associations, professional norms, piracy enforcement, and inherited beliefs about how books are sold influence adoption. Institutional theory makes those pressures analytically visible.
Why were TAM, DOI, and RBV not selected?	TAM focuses more on individual system acceptance, while this study examines firm-level strategic integration. DOI explains diffusion across adopter populations, but the TOE framework already captures many innovation attributes while offering organizational and environmental depth. RBV is useful for internal capabilities but underweights external institutional and infrastructure pressures, which are central to this problem.
How will you code the data?	I will use Braun and Clarke’s six-phase thematic analysis: familiarization, initial coding, theme searching, theme review, theme definition and naming, and reporting. Coding will be inductive across the full dataset, while sensitized by TOE and the supplementary frameworks. NVivo will support organization, memoing, and audit trail documentation.
What will you do if participant claims conflict with documents?	I will treat discrepancy as data, not as error. Conflict may reveal aspirational self-presentation, uneven implementation, or environmental pressure. I will compare the evidence, record analytic memos, and avoid making unsupported claims. Contradictions will be handled through triangulation and negative case analysis.
How will you protect confidentiality?	Participants and firms will be assigned pseudonyms and case codes. Identifying details will be removed from transcripts, notes, and reports. Data will be stored securely in encrypted, password-protected systems, physical material will be locked, and re-identification keys will be stored separately. Participation will be voluntary and withdrawal will carry no penalty.
What are your delimitations?	The study is delimited to book publishing firms in Nigeria, B2B marketing strategy, executives with direct responsibility for B2B marketing or IT-enabled sales, and organizational experience rather than technical evaluation of IT systems. These boundaries keep the study aligned, feasible, and focused.
What are your limitations?	Limitations include self-report and recall bias, participation bias, lack of statistical generalizability, insider positionality, and absence of direct observation. Each has a mitigation strategy: triangulation, maximum variation, thick description, reflexivity, peer debriefing, and documentary comparison.
What is the likely contribution to practice?	The study can help publishers make better technology-investment decisions, understand how peers integrate or fail to integrate IT, and identify practical pathways from isolated digital tools to more coherent B2B account management, procurement engagement, and relationship development.
What is the likely scholarly contribution?	It provides sector-specific qualitative evidence in an underexplored African publishing context. It extends technology adoption and B2B marketing scholarship by explaining how TOE factors interact with relational and institutional forces in a creative, educational, and SME-heavy industry.
What will count as success for your dissertation study?	Success will mean producing trustworthy, well-evidenced themes that answer both research questions, show how IT is perceived and used in B2B marketing, explain integration challenges across cases, and provide actionable implications for executives and policy stakeholders without overstating transferability.

Segment	Script
Opening	Good morning, Chair, committee members, and faculty. My name is Gbadega Adedapo, a DBA student at Westcliff University. I appreciate the opportunity to present my preliminary defense covering Chapters One to Three of my dissertation titled “The Role of Information Technology Use in B2B Marketing Strategies: A Qualitative Multiple-Case Study of the Nigerian Book Publishing Industry.”
Problem and purpose	The core problem I am addressing is that Nigerian book publishing firms operate in an increasingly digital institutional market, yet many still rely on analogue, relationship-driven B2B marketing practices. The purpose of my study is to explore how publishing executives perceive the role of information technology in B2B marketing and the challenges they experience when integrating it.
Research questions	The study is guided by two research questions. First, how do publishing executives describe the role of IT in their B2B marketing strategies? Second, what challenges do they experience when integrating IT into those strategies? These questions are intentionally exploratory because the study seeks depth of explanation, not numerical prediction.
Framework	The primary framework is the technology-organization-environment framework. I supplement it with relationship marketing theory because publishing sales depend on trust and commitment, and with institutional theory because regulation, piracy, norms, and procurement practices shape adoption decisions.
Literature gap	The literature shows that Nigerian publishing is a relational B2B industry and that digital tools are present but often partially integrated. However, the literature remains limited in explaining how executives reason through digital adoption, how TOE factors interact in practice, and what firms actually attempt when moving from partial adoption to strategic integration.
Methodology	To address this gap, I propose a qualitative multiple case study. Each firm will be treated as a bounded case. I will collect evidence from 12 to 18 executives across approximately six organizations, 24 to 36 firm-produced documents, and 18 to 30 external procurement and industry records.
Analysis and trustworthiness	Data will be analyzed using Braun and Clarke’s six-phase thematic analysis in NVivo, first within each case and then across cases. Trustworthiness will be strengthened through triangulation, thick description, audit trail, reflexive journaling, participant validation of interpretive summaries, peer debriefing, and negative case attention.
Closing	My study is designed to contribute applied DBA knowledge by helping Nigerian publishing executives understand how to move from isolated digital activity to coherent IT-enabled B2B marketing strategy. I respectfully welcome your questions and recommendations for strengthening the study before data collection.
